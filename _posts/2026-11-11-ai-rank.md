**TL;DR:** How should an AI-SQL engine execute semantic top-k with LLMs? Our experiments show that pointwise ranking beats pairwise comparison by both accuracy and latency!
## 1. Introduction
One of the most useful AI-SQL operations is semantic top-k: given a question and a collection of records, return top k records that best match this question. For example, a sales team might want to find calls that most clearly show a customer's commitment to purchase; a lawyer might want to find passages that best support a legal argument.

There are multiple approaches to find the top k items in a dataset according to some criteria, but which approach works best for typical, business-relevant data processing tasks? Surprisingly, we find that the simpler and cheaper pointwise scoring approach is also more accurate than pairwise comparison. 

In this blog post, we discuss how we explored different approaches to implement semantic top-k task in [Quail](https://github.com/fsdatalab/quail), our open-source SQL engine that supports AI functions.

We'll go through:

- Pointwise scoring 
- Pairwise comparison 
- Our experimental results 
- Why pairwise comparison performs worse 
## 2. Embedding-based Retrieval
One established approach is embedding-based retrieval. It computes the embeddings of questions and records separately, then ranks by cosine similarity between them. However, LOTUS[^1] pointed out that embedding-based retrieval performed well on relevance-based ranking but poorly on reasoning-based ranking. When similarity does not align with the natural language criteria, the accuracy of embedding-based retrieval can drop. Instead, we can use LLMs to evaluate records directly against the natural language ranking criteria.
## 3. Pairwise Comparison
Another approach to implement semantic top-k is using pairwise LLM comparisons and a quick-select top-k algorithm. More specifically, the algorithm selects a pivot record, compares it with other records, and repeatedly removes records that cannot be in the top k.

We wanted to use pairwise comparison for Quail's semantic top-k since it is conventionally considered better at accuracy. LOTUS reported that its pairwise-based top-k outperformed pointwise ranking on SciFact (0.765 vs. 0.457 nDCG@10) and HellaSwag-bench (0.919 vs. 0.091). Qin et al.[^2] also found that pairwise method outperforms pointwise method by more than 10% on average nDCG@10. 
## 4. Pointwise Scoring
The existing approach to implement semantic top-k is by reusing `AI.SCORE` function that Quail already supports. More specifically, it scores each record, sorts the score, and returns the top k records. Here is an example query for a sales team wants to know top 25 sales calls with commitments to buy the product:

```SQL
SELECT c.id, AI.SCORE(PROMPT(<SALES_COMMITS>, c.transcript)) AS commit_score
FROM sales_calls c
WHERE c.deal_stage = 'Negotiation'
ORDER BY commit_score DESC, c.id
LIMIT 25
```

## 5. Pointwise Beats Pairwise
We ran experiments with Qwen3-4B to compare pointwise scoring and pairwise comparison on three business-relevant semantic top-k tasks:
- Sales: ranking sales calls by how close customers are to committing to a purchase
- Customer Support: ranking customer messages by how strongly they express frustration or dissatisfaction with the agent
- Legal:[^3]: ranking legal passages by how strongly they support a court's consideration of an argument raised late or not preserved

We also included two baselines: Reranker uses Qwen3-Reranker-4B to do pointwise scoring, while Retrieval uses Qwen3-Embedding-4B to do embedding-based retrieval.

**To our surprise, pointwise scoring achieves higher accuracy than pairwise comparison on all three tasks, while running 4.7x faster in total and 5.8x faster per task (geometric mean).**[^4]

More LLM calls don't necessarily buy accuracy. In another study of semantic top-k, Lenzi et al.[^5] compared two ranking strategies and found that the one requiring more LLM calls generally incurred higher costs without consistently achieving better accuracy.
## 6. Why?
In this section, we discuss potential reasons that make pairwise comparison less accurate than pointwise scoring.
### 6.1 Comparison Can Be Inconsistent
We observed the following two types of inconsistent comparisons during experiments.

**Models can favor a position instead of a document.** We tested Qwen3-4B and Decision-2.0-Kai-0.6B on Sales (SF=0.1) by comparing each pair of records twice, reversing the input order in the second comparison. Qwen3-4B selected the same position in both comparisons for 48.11% of same-label pairs and 4.18% of different-label pairs, while the corresponding rates of Decision-2.0-Kai-0.6B were 80.85% and 70.55%.

**First token decision can disagree with final decision.** Some pairwise comparisons may require reasoning. To reduce latency, Quail scores the first token of the answer without generating the full response. We found that when allowed to generate the full response, Qwen3-4B's first-token decision differed from the final decision 28.89% of times on Sales (SF=0.1).

### 6.2 How Can Inconsistent Comparison Affect Top-k Results?
Two properties of our pairwise approach can amplify the impact of inconsistent comparisons.

**Comparison errors can accumulate in Quickselect.**  Although comparing two records directly may make ranking easier by giving LLMs more context, errors can accumulate across multiple comparisons. In our randomized quickselect, records that remain in the candidate set may be compared against several pivots in successive rounds. A single incorrect comparison can cause a true top-k record to be discarded.

**Pairwise comparisons lose the fine-grained information that scores give.** Pointwise gives score, while pairwise only gives win/loss, discarding how strong the model favors one choice over the other.[^6] As a result, an uncertain comparison can have the same impact as a confident one.
## 7. Conclusion
In this article, we walked through several approaches to semantic top-k: pointwise scoring and pairwise comparison. Given the experiment results, we decide to implement semantic top-k by reusing Quail's existing `AI.SCORE` function instead of adding a dedicated `AI.RANK` operator.[^7]
